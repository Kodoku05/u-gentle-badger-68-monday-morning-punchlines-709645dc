Scores are editorial estimates, not results from the competition judge. Each criterion is scored 1–5 in rubric order: **M** Monday-morning relevance, **L** laugh/punchline, **S** surprise, **E** economy, **F** freshness. ## Decision and stress test

Top three: Original 13 (24), Original 2 (23), Original 1 (22). Original 12 also scores 22; Original 1 wins that tie because security confiscating a piece of the employee is a more immediate closing image than a notice-period reference.

The first implemented version was Original 11. After two deterministic runs, I rejected its generic final demand and revised the actual program to Original 13. The final joke is 114 ASCII characters and 114 UTF-8 bytes.

- Spoken-cadence read-through: the final demand is short enough to land in roughly three seconds once the séance is established. The entire joke takes longer; no recorded audio or timed audience test was performed. Editorial judgment: it earns a laugh by treating death as an ordinary employee departure.
- Surprise: pass on editorial review. A medium first appears to defeat the escape; the last line reveals that HR summoned the employee specifically to onboard the replacement.
- Stranger comprehension: pass on editorial review. Monday morning, a meeting, HR, a medium, and training a replacement establish the scene without competition context.
- Cliché check: no alarms, snoozing, coffee, weekend lament, or stock hatred of Mondays. The revised closing gives the employer a concrete demand.
- Limit: humor scores are subjective. No external audience or house-judge evaluation was run.
